Question: What is energy?
Answer: Energy is the capacity to do work or cause a physical change. Energy exists in different forms, including kinetic energy, potential energy, thermal energy, chemical energy, electrical energy, and light energy.

Question: What is the SI unit of energy?
Answer: The SI unit of energy is the joule (J). One joule is equal to one kilogram metre squared per second squared (1 J = 1 kg·m²/s²).

Question: What is electricity?
Answer: Electricity refers to phenomena associated with electric charge. In electrical circuits, electricity commonly involves the movement of electric charge through a conducting material.

Question: What is electric current?
Answer: Electric current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a point in a circuit or conductor. It is represented by the symbol I and is measured in amperes (A).

Question: What is the SI unit of electric current?
Answer: The SI unit of electric current is the ampere (A). One ampere corresponds to a flow of one coulomb of electric charge per second through a point in a circuit.

Question: What is voltage?
Answer: Voltage is the electric potential difference between two points. It represents the amount of energy transferred per unit of electric charge when charge moves between those points. Voltage is measured in volts (V).

Question: What is resistance?
Answer: Electrical resistance is the opposition a material or component provides to the flow of electric current. Resistance depends on factors such as the material, length, cross-sectional area, and temperature of a conductor.

Question: What is the SI unit of resistance?
Answer: The SI unit of electrical resistance is the ohm (Ω). According to Ohm's law, resistance is related to voltage and current by the equation R = V/I for an ohmic component under appropriate conditions.

Question: What is electrical power?
Answer: Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred, converted, or consumed. It is measured in watts (W). In a simple DC circuit, electrical power can be calculated using P = VI, where P is power, V is voltage, and I is current.

Question: What is a conductor?
Answer: A conductor is a material that allows electric charge to flow relatively easily because it contains charge carriers that can move through the material. Common electrical conductors include copper, aluminum, and silver.
