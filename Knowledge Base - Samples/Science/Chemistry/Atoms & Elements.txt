Question: What is an atom?
Answer: An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains the chemical identity and properties of that element. An atom consists of a nucleus containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by electrons.

Question: What is an element?
Answer: A chemical element is a pure substance whose atoms all have the same number of protons in their nuclei. Elements cannot be broken down into simpler substances by ordinary chemical reactions.

Question: What are the three main subatomic particles?
Answer: The three main subatomic particles are protons, neutrons, and electrons. Protons have a positive electric charge, neutrons have no electric charge, and electrons have a negative electric charge.

Question: What is a proton?
Answer: A proton is a positively charged subatomic particle found in the nucleus of an atom. The number of protons determines which chemical element an atom represents.

Question: What is a neutron?
Answer: A neutron is a subatomic particle with no net electric charge. Neutrons are found in the nucleus of most atoms and contribute to the atom's mass and nuclear stability.

Question: What is an electron?
Answer: An electron is a negatively charged subatomic particle associated with the region surrounding an atom's nucleus. Electrons occupy atomic orbitals and play a major role in chemical bonding and chemical reactions.

Question: What is the nucleus of an atom?
Answer: The nucleus is the dense central region of an atom that contains protons and neutrons. It contains nearly all of the atom's mass and has an overall positive charge because of its protons.

Question: What is the atomic number?
Answer: The atomic number is the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. It uniquely identifies a chemical element. For example, hydrogen has an atomic number of 1 because every hydrogen atom has one proton.

Question: What is the periodic table?
Answer: The periodic table is a systematic arrangement of chemical elements according to their atomic numbers and recurring chemical and physical properties. Elements are organized into periods, groups, and blocks to show relationships between their properties.

Question: What is a chemical symbol?
Answer: A chemical symbol is a standardized one- or two-letter abbreviation used to represent a chemical element. The first letter is always capitalized, while the second letter, if present, is lowercase. Examples include H for hydrogen, O for oxygen, Na for sodium, and Fe for iron.
