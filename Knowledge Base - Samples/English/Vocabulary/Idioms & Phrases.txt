Question: What is an idiom?
Answer: An idiom is a commonly used expression whose meaning cannot always be understood by interpreting the individual words literally. Idioms often have a figurative meaning that is understood through common usage and context.

Question: What does the idiom "break the ice" mean?
Answer: "Break the ice" means to start a conversation or social interaction and help people feel more relaxed or comfortable in an unfamiliar or new situation.

Question: What does the idiom "piece of cake" mean?
Answer: "Piece of cake" means that something is very easy to do or accomplish.

Question: What does the idiom "hit the nail on the head" mean?
Answer: "Hit the nail on the head" means to describe, identify, or explain something exactly and correctly.

Question: What does the idiom "once in a blue moon" mean?
Answer: "Once in a blue moon" means something happens very rarely or only on unusual occasions.

Question: What does the idiom "under the weather" mean?
Answer: "Under the weather" means feeling unwell, sick, or not in good health.

Question: What does the idiom "spill the beans" mean?
Answer: "Spill the beans" means to reveal a secret or confidential information, often unintentionally or before it was supposed to be revealed.

Question: What does the idiom "a blessing in disguise" mean?
Answer: "A blessing in disguise" refers to something that initially appears to be bad, disappointing, or unfortunate but later turns out to have a positive or beneficial result.

Question: What does the idiom "cost an arm and a leg" mean?
Answer: "Cost an arm and a leg" means that something is extremely expensive or costs a very large amount of money.

Question: What does the idiom "burn the midnight oil" mean?
Answer: "Burn the midnight oil" means to work, study, or continue doing something late into the night.

Question: What does the idiom "when pigs fly" mean?
Answer: "When pigs fly" is used to say that something is extremely unlikely to happen or will probably never happen.
