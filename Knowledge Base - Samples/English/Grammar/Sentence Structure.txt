Question: What is a sentence?
Answer: A sentence is a group of words that expresses a complete thought and normally contains a subject and a predicate. A complete sentence typically has a finite verb and follows the grammatical rules of the language.

Question: What are the main parts of a sentence?
Answer: The two main structural parts of a sentence are the subject and the predicate. The subject identifies who or what the sentence is about, while the predicate provides information about the subject, usually including the verb and its related words.

Question: What is a subject?
Answer: The subject is the person, place, thing, or idea that a sentence is about. In an active sentence, the subject commonly performs the action expressed by the verb. For example, in the sentence "The student reads a book," the subject is "The student."

Question: What is a predicate?
Answer: The predicate is the part of a sentence that tells what the subject does, what the subject is, or what happens to the subject. It normally contains the verb and may include objects, complements, and other modifiers.

Question: What is a simple sentence?
Answer: A simple sentence contains one independent clause and expresses a complete thought. It may have a compound subject or compound predicate but does not contain multiple independent clauses. Example: "The children played outside."

Question: What is a compound sentence?
Answer: A compound sentence contains two or more independent clauses. The clauses are commonly joined by a coordinating conjunction such as and, but, or, so, or by appropriate punctuation such as a semicolon. Example: "The children played outside, and their parents watched them."

Question: What is a complex sentence?
Answer: A complex sentence contains one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. The dependent clause cannot normally stand alone as a complete sentence. Example: "The children played outside because the weather was pleasant."

Question: What is subject-verb agreement?
Answer: Subject-verb agreement is the grammatical rule that requires a verb to agree with its subject in number and, in some cases, person. For example, "She works" uses a singular subject with a singular verb, while "They work" uses a plural subject with the corresponding verb form.

Question: What is a clause?
Answer: A clause is a group of words that contains a subject and a verb or verb phrase. Clauses can be independent and express a complete thought, or dependent and require another clause to form a complete sentence.

Question: What is a phrase?
Answer: A phrase is a group of related words that functions as a unit within a sentence but does not contain both a subject and a finite verb forming a complete clause. Examples include "in the garden," "the tall building," and "very quickly."
